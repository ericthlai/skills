# Worked example — a vendor work-up (2026-06-03)

A real `company/vendor` `work-up` run as meeting prep. Shows the target shape:
recommendation-first, cited, multi-round, self-correcting. The full packet is filed under
the packets directory with the vendor named — a scout packet is *about* its subject, so it names it.
This teaching copy does not: the lesson is the method, and nothing in it needs an identity.

**Input:** "Research the three people, the company, and their proposal for Friday's meeting; I want
to be skeptical and prepared, and deliver a prep note to a firm leader." (Decision verb + vendor →
`work-up`.)

**Decision question:** Is this vendor genuinely additive to our AI program, or duplicative — and
what posture and questions get a real evaluation rather than a sales pitch?

**Recommendation:** Treat the meeting as an information session, not a buying conversation; hold
at "explore." The advisory scope overlapped our internal AI lead heavily, so the burden was on
them to show what an external advisor adds.

**How the rounds went — the self-correction is the point:**

- **Round 1 (shallow):** company basics and a principal's title, from search snippets only.
  Concluded "their flagship product is consumer-goods focused, poor fit for our sector."
  *Too quick.*
- **Round 2:** fetched their industry vertical page → revised. They *do* have a practice in our
  sector, but it targets a different buyer within it than us.
- **Round 3 (deep primary-page sweep — about, clients, a named case study, reviews, people):**
  the flagship method is teaching two commodity AI tools; **no named product customers in our
  sector**; a formidable ex-Big-4 consulting arm. Verdict: the capability is in-house; what is
  actually sold is capacity, facilitation, and outside credibility around a basic methodology.

**Lessons this example encodes:**

- Fetch primary pages early. The sector vertical was a link in the *first* search results and was
  missed for two rounds.
- Sweep the people, and name what you could not verify. One principal stayed `[unverified]` and
  was reported that way rather than dropped.
- The two-source rule has teeth: a "~20k stars" snippet was corrected to 27.5k against the primary
  repository in a sibling run.
- Append rounds; never regenerate. How the read evolved is itself the evidence.
- Separate the offerings — advisory versus product — and ask "additive versus what we can build
  in-house."
